## Goal

Two things exist when this is done. First, every finishing pass ends with a plain-language recap, the same block at the top of the close-out status and in the final Chapter, that quotes the plan's Goal, states in plain words what the tree does now, lists the refinements made during the run (each spec amendment, each appended section, each ruling the operator gave mid-run), and names what waits on the operator, so the operator can check the result against the intent without reading the Chapters. Second, a spec is read adversarially against its own Goal before it is armed: a fresh-context, read-only `plan-reviewer` agent, dispatched by the brainstorming skill after the author's self-review and the blind read, returns severity-ranked findings under a fixed set of questions (an acceptance clause satisfiable by a reading nobody wants, an instruction an implementer could read two ways, a surface outside the scope lists the change will falsify, a contradiction with a rule the executor will load, an inter-section assumption no acceptance guarantees, an author preference written as the operator's ruling), and the author adjudicates each into the spec, into `## Assumptions`, or into a decision ask before the plan is written as `Ready` or armed. A scenario probe holds the dispatch moment so a later wording change that drops the reviewer is a finding rather than a surprise.

## Approach

The recap is a required block with a fixed shape, owned by finishing-work's step 5 beside the assumptions block it already mandates there, because that step is the one place the whole effort is read at once and the close-out status is the surface the operator actually reads. The block is written in the client-briefing register: it quotes the plan's `## Goal` paragraph, states the as-built result in plain words with no internal identifier unresolved, lists the refinements as one line each (a spec amendment recorded in a Chapter, a section appended under the out-of-scope route, an operator ruling taken mid-run, a Decision reversed), and closes with the operator-pending items. It rides in the final Chapter as well so it survives in the archive, and its source is the Chapters rather than the author's memory: the refinements are read off the Chapters' `Decisions / Surprises` lines, which is where executing-work already records every spec amendment, so a run that recorded its Chapters honestly produces the list mechanically and one that did not is caught by a recap that cannot be written.

The plan reviewer is a new read-only charter in the strict class the read-only guard governs, dispatched at fable and effort high through Workflow's `agent()` on executing-work's Reviewer Dispatch template, the route finishing-work's lenses take and the one the doctrine's standing Workflow grant covers (a read-only agent whose effort the Agent tool cannot set). It reads the spec whole, the Goal and Decisions first, then the sections against them, and then the repository where a claim depends on it (a `Files in scope:` list against the surfaces that speak the contract, an acceptance clause against the test or command it names). Its brief carries the spec path alone, never the design conversation, on the same ground the blind-reader's charter states: the intent story is what the author already has and what the reviewer must not. Its questions are a closed set of six, each producing a tagged finding, so the author adjudicates by question and the recap can count them. The dispatch sits in brainstorming step 10 after the blind read, because the two answer different questions and the blind read's gating-definition litmus feeds the reviewer's two-way-reading question, and before the spec is written `Ready` or `In Progress`, because a finding found after arming is rework and one found after execution is a Chapter line. The author adjudicates each finding three ways, the same three the blind read uses, and records `plan review: <n> findings, <a> fixed in the spec, <b> assumed, <c> asked` beside the blind-read line in the handoff recap. A trivial spec that skipped the blind read skips this too and says so; a spec authored by a session that cannot dispatch (a worker under an external engine) records the skip in `## Assumptions` and the engine's own review stands in.

The charter is the deliverable and is written once, so the six questions are the design decision recorded here and not left to the section: (1) **unwanted satisfaction**, an acceptance criterion that a reading nobody wants would satisfy, or that no run performs; (2) **two-way instruction**, a sentence a `sonnet` implementer with the section text alone could read two ways, with both readings stated; (3) **falsified surface**, a file, document, test or pinned copy outside every `Files in scope:` list and `## Out of Scope` that the change as written would make false, found by reading the repository and not by asking the author; (4) **rule conflict**, an instruction that contradicts a doctrine bullet, a skill rule or a charter line the executor will have loaded, named by its bold lead; (5) **unguaranteed hand-off**, a thing section N assumes section N-1 produced that N-1's acceptance does not guarantee, or an ordering the sections need and the header does not state; (6) **preference as ruling**, a Decision or Assumption that records the author's pick in the operator's voice, or a decision the operator would want to make written as settled. The reviewer rates a finding by what following the spec as written would cost: Critical where the Goal would not be achieved, Major where a section would ship something the Goal did not ask for or a reviewer would send back, Minor otherwise; it names the passage, states the reading that fails, and proposes the sentence that closes it only where one sentence does, so the author's fix stays a deletion or a narrowing rather than a paragraph.

## Decisions

Decided 2026-09-08 by the Expert seat, recorded for the operator's approval at arming, on the sketch the operator approved over the relay channel ("I think this is outstanding").

1. **The recap's source is the Chapters, not the author's recollection.** The refinements list is read off the `Decisions / Surprises` lines, so it is a mechanical read of a record executing-work already mandates, and a recap that cannot be assembled from them is a signal that a Chapter was written thin. The alternative, a free summary, is exactly the kind of prose that reads well and omits the one refinement the operator would have questioned.
2. **The plan reviewer is a new charter, not a mode of the adversarial-reviewer or the blind-reader.** The adversarial-reviewer reads a diff against a spec and the blind-reader reads a document for comprehension without the intent story; the plan reviewer needs the Goal (which is the intent story) and no diff, so it is a third subject, and folding it into either charter would widen a charter whose `tools:` line is a privilege grant for a different moment.
3. **The six questions are closed and live in the charter.** An open-ended "find flaws in this plan" returns a survey; a closed set returns findings the author can adjudicate by kind and the recap can count. The set is closed at six and any seventh is a plan of its own.
4. **The dispatch is after the blind read and before `Ready`, at fable and high through Workflow.** After, because the litmus over gating definitions feeds question 2 and a spec rewritten after the litmus is what the reviewer should read. Fable, because this is design-time judgment, the axis on which the tiers diverge (operator-tier memory `model-tier-substitution-for-review`), so the substitution ladder that is safe for code review is not safe here and the charter says to wait rather than drop.
5. **The reviewer reads the repository, not only the spec.** Question 3 cannot be answered from the text, and the review-loop-exit plan's missed documents are the case: they were findable by grep at authoring.
6. **No numeric bar on findings and no re-dispatch loop.** One dispatch per spec; the author adjudicates and ships. A spec whose review returns a Critical is rewritten and may be re-dispatched once at the author's judgment; the loop is bounded by the same terminal condition executing-work states, read at the class of what remains.

## Out of Scope

- A reviewer for plans authored by an external engine's worker, which does not brainstorm; that engine's review stands in, and the brainstorming passage says so.
- A re-dispatch loop over the plan review beyond the author's one optional re-run after a Critical (Decision 6).
- Retrofitting the recap onto archived plans; the block starts with the next plan to close, this one included.
- Changing the blind-reader's charter or its litmus; the plan review sits after it and consumes its output.
- A recap in the per-section Chapters; the recap is a whole-effort read and lives at the close.
